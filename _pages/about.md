Jesse van Remmerden is a third-year PhD student whose research centers on deep reinforcement learning and other deep learning approaches for scheduling problems. He is supervised by Zaharah Bukhsh and Yingqian Zhang. His work focuses on offline RL for scheduling problems (e.g., job-shop and flexible job-shop scheduling), leveraging action masking, distributional critics, and conservative policy learning to ensure feasible, data-efficient decisions. He has developed methods that are able to learn SOTA scheduling policies solely through sub-optimal data—even when those examples are generated through a random heuristic—and demonstrated that robust policies can be learned without simulated environments. Beyond classical scheduling, his research extends to multi-objective decision-making for infrastructural maintenance planning, where models trade off cost, risk, and service levels and are validated on real-world assets. Across projects, he emphasizes reproducibility and practical deployment, releasing implementations and benchmarks to support reliable evaluation and adoption.
