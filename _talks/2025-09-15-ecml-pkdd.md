I presented [Offline-LD](/publication/offline-ld-2025) in the **Journal Track** of ECML PKDD 2025, the European Conference on Machine Learning and Principles and Practice of Knowledge Discovery in Databases, held in Porto from 15–19 September 2025. The paper appeared in *Machine Learning* (Springer) and was selected for presentation at the conference through the journal track.

The talk covered Offline-LD, the first fully end-to-end offline RL approach for the Job Shop Scheduling Problem: how maskable variants of Quantile Regression DQN and discrete Soft Actor–Critic can be trained through Conservative Q-Learning to learn dispatching policies from historical schedules alone, with no simulator, and why the resulting policies outperform online RL baselines while training on only 100 constraint-programming solutions.

The finding that drew most discussion was that deliberately adding noise to the expert dataset did not degrade performance, and in places improved it — which suggests these methods are more robust to the imperfections of real industrial scheduling logs than the offline RL literature's emphasis on data quality would predict.
